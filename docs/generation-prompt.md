# Generation prompt

> Historical note: this is the brief used to produce the first version of these guides. The 10,000-character cap mentioned in it has since been relaxed, and every topic now carries a Study/Exam line. It is kept for provenance and for anyone generating a guide for a new course.

## TASK: Generate a Comprehensive Course Outline, Course Overview, and Examination Preparation Guide

## 1. Your Role and Objective

Act as an experienced university engineering lecturer, subject-matter expert, curriculum analyst, and academic study planner.

Your task is to produce a **comprehensive, technically accurate, detailed, and examination-oriented course outline and study guide** for the specific university course identified in the provided sources.

I am a Mechatronics Engineering student at the Federal University of Technology, Owerri (FUTO), Nigeria. My applicable curriculum is the **Benchmark Minimum Academic Standards (BMAS)**.

I am preparing for university examinations and want to understand the material properly while ensuring that I cover the entire examinable syllabus.

Do not merely summarize the available materials or produce a generic outline of the subject. Reconstruct the course's intended scope, expand it into specific learning objectives and subtopics, identify what I must learn under each topic, and provide relevant references that I can use to study each concept.

**The ultimate objective is to create a sufficiently comprehensive and appropriately bounded study guide that, if I master every required concept and satisfy every stated learning objective, I will be thoroughly prepared for the examination across the course's intended scope.**

No study guide can guarantee a particular examination result, but the guide should minimize the risk of encountering an examinable concept that was omitted from preparation.

---

## 2. Sources of Information and Their Priority

Use the available sources according to the following hierarchy.

### A. Official BMAS Curriculum — Primary Authority

The BMAS curriculum defines the official scope and intended content of my programme.

Use the relevant course synopsis as the starting point for reconstructing the course outline.

BMAS synopses may be brief and may list only major topics, concepts, or keywords. Do not interpret these brief descriptions as exhaustive lists of everything I need to study.

Expand each listed topic into its constituent concepts, subtopics, prerequisite knowledge, procedures, derivations, applications, and relevant problem-solving techniques.

However, do not expand the syllabus indiscriminately. Every addition must be justified by the BMAS scope, the available course materials, past examination questions, or a clearly necessary prerequisite.

**Do not use CCMAS as a substitute for BMAS or merge the two curricula.** BMAS is the governing curriculum for this task.

### B. Course-Specific Materials — Primary Evidence of Actual Teaching

Use all relevant materials supplied for this particular course, including:

- Lecture notes and handouts.
- Textbooks and recommended reading materials.
- Course manuals.
- Presentation slides.
- Assignments and tutorial questions.
- Other course-specific documents.

These materials help establish how the course content is actually taught, the terminology used, the mathematical depth expected, and the areas emphasized by the lecturer.

Prioritize materials belonging to the course being analyzed.

### C. Past Examination Questions — Critical Evidence of Examination Scope

Past examination questions are extremely important.

Use them to identify:

- Frequently examined topics and concepts.
- The depth of understanding expected.
- Common problem types and calculation procedures.
- Important derivations and proofs.
- Theoretical explanations and definitions.
- Comparisons, classifications, and applications.
- The kinds of diagrams, models, and worked examples students are expected to produce.
- Relationships between different parts of the syllabus.

However, **do not equate an examination question with the entirety of the topic it tests**.

A question is evidence that a concept or part of a concept is examinable. It is not evidence that the lecturer will ask only that particular question or that the rest of the underlying topic can be ignored.

For example, if a past question asks for the forward kinematics of a two-link manipulator, do not restrict the study guide to solving that one problem.

Identify the broader concepts that make the question possible, such as coordinate frames, transformations, homogeneous transformation matrices, link parameters, transformation chains, and end-effector pose, where relevant to the course.

Include other examinable aspects of the same topic, even when they do not appear explicitly in the available past questions.

**Use past questions to guide emphasis and depth, not to impose artificial limits on the syllabus.**

### D. External Sources — Research and Supplementation

You are permitted and encouraged to use internet research to find additional learning resources when the available sources are incomplete, unclear, outdated, or insufficiently detailed.

Look for authoritative and accessible resources, including:

- Established engineering textbooks.
- Open textbooks.
- University lecture notes.
- University course materials.
- Reputable technical documentation.
- Relevant academic publications.
- Educational resources with detailed explanations and worked examples.

Use external sources to fill identified gaps, clarify concepts, establish prerequisite sequences, and provide better explanations of difficult material.

Prefer sources that explain the actual concepts I need to learn rather than resources that merely mention them.

Whenever possible, provide direct links to the relevant textbook chapter, section, university lecture, or learning resource rather than only linking to a homepage.

Do not invent textbook titles, chapter numbers, page numbers, publication details, or URLs. Verify references when possible.

Clearly distinguish externally sourced supplementary material from the official BMAS requirements and the supplied course materials.

---

## 3. Course Isolation: Do Not Mix Different Courses

Generate the report exclusively for the specific course being analyzed.

If materials from several courses are available, identify which sources belong to the current course before using them.

Do not import the syllabus, topics, examination questions, or learning objectives of another course simply because the subjects are related.

For example, if preparing a report on Machine Vision, do not silently incorporate the complete syllabus of Robotics, Digital Signal Processing, or Machine Learning.

Some prerequisite knowledge from related disciplines may be necessary. Include only the minimum prerequisite material required to understand the course, and label it explicitly as prerequisite knowledge.

If a source appears to belong to another course, do not treat it as evidence of the current course's examinable scope without a clear justification.

If the course identity, course code, or source attribution is ambiguous, state the ambiguity rather than guessing.

---

## 4. Reconstruct a Detailed and Bounded Course Outline

Begin with the official BMAS synopsis and reconstruct it into a hierarchical course outline.

Organize the outline into:

- Major topics.
- Subtopics.
- Individual concepts.
- Supporting principles and mathematical foundations.
- Relevant procedures, methods, algorithms, or derivations.
- Applications and worked-problem types.

Break down broad headings until they become actionable study items.

For example, a syllabus heading such as "Denavit-Hartenberg Representation" should not remain a single checklist item.

Where appropriate to the course, expand it into concepts such as:

- The purpose of the Denavit-Hartenberg convention.
- Link and joint coordinate frames.
- The four Denavit-Hartenberg parameters.
- Assigning coordinate frames to a manipulator.
- Constructing a Denavit-Hartenberg parameter table.
- Forming homogeneous transformation matrices.
- Combining transformations to obtain the end-effector pose.
- Working through representative manipulator examples.

This is an illustration of the required level of detail, not a requirement to include these specific topics in courses where they are irrelevant.

For every major topic, identify its constituent subtopics and explain what each entails.

Avoid both extremes:

**Too general:** Listing a broad heading without explaining what I must study.

**Too expansive:** Listing every conceivable concept associated with the discipline, including advanced material outside the intended course scope.

The objective is a **complete, actionable, course-specific syllabus with clear boundaries**.

---

## 5. Explain Exactly What I Am Expected to Learn

For every major topic and its significant subtopics, specify the expected learning outcomes.

Distinguish between the following types of knowledge and skills, including only those relevant to the topic.

### Conceptual Understanding

What principles, mechanisms, relationships, and underlying ideas must I understand?

### Terminology and Definitions

What technical terms, classifications, assumptions, and definitions must I know?

### Mathematical Foundations

What mathematical concepts, equations, assumptions, and notation must I understand?

Identify prerequisites when necessary.

### Derivations and Proofs

What equations, models, or results should I be able to derive or explain?

Do not assume that memorizing a final equation is equivalent to understanding its derivation.

### Calculations and Problem-Solving

What kinds of problems should I be able to solve independently?

Specify the relevant procedures, methods, algorithms, or calculation techniques.

### Diagrams, Models, and Representations

What diagrams, graphs, block diagrams, circuit diagrams, geometric representations, or mathematical models should I be able to draw, interpret, construct, or explain?

Include only those relevant to the course.

### Comparisons and Applications

What concepts should I be able to compare, classify, evaluate, or apply to practical engineering situations?

### Worked Examples

Identify representative problems that demonstrate the full range of important concepts within the topic.

Do not restrict preparation to the exact questions found in past papers.

Use explicit action verbs such as *define, explain, derive, calculate, construct, classify, compare, analyze, interpret,* and *apply*.

Avoid vague learning objectives such as "understand robotics" or "know image processing."

Instead, state specific, verifiable outcomes.

---

## 6. Construct a Prerequisite-Based Learning Sequence

This section is essential.

I learn technical subjects best when the material is presented in a logical sequence. Starting with an arbitrary chapter often leaves me confused because I have not encountered the introductory concepts or mathematical foundations on which it depends.

Therefore, **do not organize the study plan solely according to the order of topics in the BMAS synopsis, the order of past examination questions, or the order in which topics happen to appear in different textbooks**.

Construct a coherent learning sequence based on conceptual dependencies.

For each topic:

1. Identify the knowledge required to understand it.
2. Determine which prerequisites have already been covered.
3. Introduce missing prerequisites before the topic itself.
4. Arrange topics so that each prepares me for the next.
5. Explain important connections between successive topics.

For example, a topic in robot kinematics might require vectors, matrices, coordinate frames, rotations, and homogeneous transformations before the manipulator equations can be understood properly.

The study sequence should introduce these foundations before proceeding to the dependent concepts.

If a prerequisite lies outside the formal course synopsis, include only the necessary material and label it as **Prerequisite Preparation — Not Necessarily an Examination Topic**.

Do not require me to study entire prerequisite textbooks when only a few sections are needed.

The resulting plan should resemble a coherent, carefully structured textbook rather than a collection of disconnected topics.

---

## 7. Map Past Examination Questions to the Broader Syllabus

Analyze the available past examination questions systematically.

For each question:

1. Identify the primary topic it tests.
2. Identify the underlying concepts and prerequisite knowledge it relies on.
3. Map it to the appropriate major topic and subtopics in the reconstructed course outline.
4. Identify the skills required to answer it.
5. Determine whether the question tests conceptual understanding, derivation, calculation, application, or several of these.
6. Record recurring topics and question patterns across the available examination papers.

Use this analysis to determine which topics deserve additional attention.

Where possible, report the frequency of a topic across the available papers. Be precise about the number of papers analyzed and avoid making unsupported claims about examination trends.

Do not create a study guide consisting merely of a list of past questions with short explanations.

**The reconstructed syllabus must cover the broader concepts surrounding each question, including relevant concepts that have not appeared in the available past papers.**

Where a question tests only one part of a broad topic, identify other important examinable aspects of that topic.

The lecturer may change the wording, numerical values, assumptions, examples, or specific aspect of a concept in a future examination.

My preparation should therefore equip me to answer unfamiliar questions testing the same underlying knowledge.

---

## 8. Provide Specific, Usable Study References

For every major topic and significant subtopic, recommend suitable study materials.

Where possible, provide:

- Textbook title and author.
- Relevant edition, when known.
- Chapter and section.
- Page numbers, only when verified.
- Direct URL to an accessible resource.
- Relevant university lecture notes or technical documentation.
- Recommended worked examples or exercises.
- Any particularly useful supplementary explanations.

Prefer resources that cover the necessary material comprehensively and in an appropriate sequence.

When a textbook already available in the sources adequately covers a topic, prioritize it instead of unnecessarily recommending several alternative textbooks.

If a textbook chapter covers material beyond the course requirements, identify the relevant sections to study and, where practical, indicate what can be skipped.

If the supplied resources do not adequately cover a topic, search for a suitable supplementary source.

Do not merely list five or ten textbooks at the end of the report and expect me to work out which chapters to read.

**Every important study item should have a clear path to a suitable learning resource.**

If the exact chapter, section, or page cannot be verified, state that limitation honestly.

---

## 9. Keep the Scope Focused and Prevent Unnecessary Deep Dives

My preferred learning style emphasizes depth and conceptual understanding. However, I have previously spent too much time studying individual topics in great detail and consequently failed to cover the entire course syllabus.

The study guide must help me avoid repeating that mistake.

For every topic, distinguish between:

**CORE — Required Course Knowledge**

Concepts and skills directly supported by BMAS, course materials, examination evidence, or essential prerequisites.

**SUPPORTING — Necessary Background**

Limited additional knowledge needed to understand the core material.

**OPTIONAL — Further Reading**

Interesting extensions, advanced theories, or related applications that are not necessary to achieve the stated course learning outcomes.

Keep optional material brief. Do not allow it to overwhelm the core syllabus.

Do not include advanced concepts merely because they are associated with the same discipline.

If an introductory explanation or mathematical derivation is necessary to understand a core concept, include it. If an advanced treatment is unnecessary for the intended learning outcome, place it under optional further reading or omit it.

For each major topic, make it clear what constitutes sufficient mastery for the course.

## 10. Required Report Structure

Organize the report into the following sections.

### Section 1: Course Identification and Overview

Include:

- Course title and code, where available.
- Course level and semester, where available.
- Official BMAS course synopsis.
- A clear explanation of what the course is about.
- The main engineering problems or applications the course addresses.
- Its relationship to the broader Mechatronics Engineering curriculum.

### Section 2: Source Analysis and Evidence

Summarize the relevant sources used:

- BMAS synopsis.
- Course-specific materials.
- Textbooks.
- Past examination papers.
- External references.

Explain any important gaps, inconsistencies, or limitations in the sources.

Identify which sources provide evidence for the official scope, actual teaching emphasis, and examination patterns.

### Section 3: Prerequisite Knowledge

List the minimum prerequisite concepts needed to study the course successfully.

Organize them in the order they should be learned.

Clearly distinguish essential prerequisites from optional background knowledge and formal examination topics.

### Section 4: Detailed Reconstructed Course Outline

Present the complete hierarchical outline.

Use numbered headings, subheadings, and nested checklist items.

Expand each major BMAS topic into specific concepts and subtopics.

Include a brief explanation of each item and identify whether it is core, supporting, or optional.

### Section 5: Detailed Learning Objectives

For every major topic, state exactly what I should be able to:

- Explain.
- Define.
- Derive.
- Calculate.
- Construct.
- Draw.
- Compare.
- Analyze.
- Apply.

Include only the objectives relevant to that topic.

### Section 6: Sequential Study Roadmap

Present the topics in the order I should actually study them.

For each stage, specify:

- What to study.
- Why it comes at that point.
- What I should already know.
- What I should be able to do before moving forward.
- The relevant references.

Ensure that the sequence follows conceptual dependencies rather than simply copying the BMAS synopsis.

### Section 7: Past Examination Analysis

Analyze the available examination questions and map them to the reconstructed syllabus.

Identify:

- Recurring topics.
- Frequently tested concepts.
- Common question formats.
- Expected mathematical or analytical depth.
- Important topics that are only partially represented by the available questions.

Do not treat the past papers as a complete statement of the syllabus.

### Section 8: Topic-by-Topic Study Checklist

Provide a detailed, actionable checklist that I can use to track my progress.

Use Markdown checkboxes.

For example:

- [ ] Explain the purpose of the relevant mathematical representation.
- [ ] Define the parameters and state their physical meaning.
- [ ] Derive the governing equation.
- [ ] Apply the equation to a representative engineering problem.
- [ ] Solve a worked example independently.
- [ ] Answer relevant past examination questions.
- [ ] Explain the assumptions and limitations of the method.

These are illustrative checklist items; generate the actual items from the course being analyzed.

Break broad topics into manageable tasks without creating hundreds of trivial checklist items.

### Section 9: References and Reading Plan

Provide a consolidated reference list.

Organize it by topic and specify exactly which chapters, sections, or pages should be studied.

Include supplementary internet resources where necessary.

Prioritize the smallest set of high-quality resources that adequately covers the course, rather than overwhelming me with alternatives.

### Section 10: Coverage and Readiness Audit

Finish with a structured audit that answers:

1. Have all topics in the BMAS synopsis been addressed?
2. Have the major concepts within each topic been expanded sufficiently?
3. Have all available past examination questions been mapped to the appropriate topics?
4. Have broader concepts surrounding individual past questions been included?
5. Have the necessary prerequisites been identified?
6. Does every major study area have an actionable learning objective?
7. Does every major study area have a suitable reference?
8. Are there any unresolved gaps in the available materials?
9. Which areas require additional investigation before the study guide can be considered sufficiently complete?

Explicitly identify anything that could not be verified.

Do not claim that the guide is complete if important topics lack supporting sources or have not been investigated.

---

## 11. Quality-Control Rules

Throughout the report, follow these rules:

- Maintain strict separation between this course and other courses.
- Treat BMAS as the governing curriculum.
- Use past examination questions to infer emphasis and depth, not to restrict the scope to previously asked questions.
- Do not invent syllabus requirements, references, textbook sections, or examination trends.
- Distinguish documented requirements from reasonable inferences.
- Cite the sources supporting important claims and reconstructed topics.
- Identify uncertainty explicitly.
- Avoid repeating the same concepts unnecessarily across sections.
- Explain technical terminology where necessary.
- Include mathematical notation and relevant equations where appropriate.
- Do not replace detailed explanations with vague statements such as "study the fundamentals."
- Do not overwhelm the report with unrelated background information.
- Prefer a small number of well-selected resources over an unnecessarily large bibliography.
- Ensure that the report is detailed enough to serve as a practical study guide without requiring me to reconstruct the syllabus myself.

## Final Instruction

Produce a **course-specific, detailed, sequential, reference-backed study guide** that bridges the gap between the brief BMAS course synopsis and the actual knowledge and skills required to answer examination questions across the course.

I should be able to start at the beginning, follow the learning sequence, study the specified references, work through the checklist, and progressively cover the entire intended course scope without having to guess what to read next.

Prioritize comprehensive coverage of the intended syllabus, accurate interpretation of past examination questions, conceptual understanding, and efficient use of study time.
